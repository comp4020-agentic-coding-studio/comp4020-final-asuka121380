# C8 reflection — From inspecting a model to building in a place

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was recognising that my project should feel like building within an environment, not manipulating a model against an empty background. The first version could place and save bricks, but the small platform and detached controls did not deliver the experience I wanted. More functions alone would not have solved that.

I changed the direction towards a continuous setting, with the player's house becoming part of a neighbourhood. The beachfront made that idea more specific: colourful neighbours, warm sunlight and an open foreground give a small construction context without requiring a huge playable world. Moving the tools into a bottom tray and allowing fuller camera movement supported the same intention. The shift is visible between the [first workbench](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-asuka121380/commit/4b40546) and the [coastal scene](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-asuka121380/commit/57549ea).

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

It made me more conscious of the difference between specifying functionality and directing an experience. An agent can implement placement, storage and controls while still producing something that feels unlike my intention. I want to become better at identifying that gap and turning an impression into actionable requirements: keep the foreground open, make controls follow the view, and let the player place exactly the preview they see.

Atmosphere is therefore not a final decorative layer. It affects layout, camera, interaction and what I choose not to build. Equally, an attractive scene is not proof of good usability. My next step is to test whether someone unfamiliar with the project can build comfortably, and later whether cooperation adds value without making solo play dependent on others.
