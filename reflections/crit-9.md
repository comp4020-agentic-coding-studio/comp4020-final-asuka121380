# C9 reflection — Letting the original intention guide the features

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was realising that functionality and narrative need to support each other. I should begin with an intention, turn it into a design, and then implement it—not assemble a standard multiplayer framework and decide afterwards what it means.

My starting point was the memory of building LEGO with friends, or even strangers. Bricks provide the setting, but the important experience is exchanging ideas and creating something together. That helped me decide what to leave out. Text and voice chat are absent deliberately, not because I consider them impossible to implement. Instead, players can propose ideas through transparent planning bricks and draw attention to them with highlights. I want communication to happen through the construction itself.

The reference model offers a starting point, not a compulsory outcome. Imagination can take the group somewhere unexpected. Contributor records preserve who helped, while everyone can save the shared work to their own collection. These features connect cooperation with memory rather than simply making the project multiplayer.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

I want to become a developer who can explain why a feature belongs, not only how to implement it. A familiar feature is not automatically necessary, and a longer feature list does not guarantee a more coherent experience. Keeping the original intention visible gives me a reason to make choices and reject unnecessary additions.

However, intention is not evidence that the design works. Plans and highlights are my proposed shared language, not yet a proven substitute for conversation. Next, I want to observe whether real players can understand one another and develop ideas together through them.
